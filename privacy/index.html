Kosh privacy policy

Effective 29 October 2026 · Kosh for Android, published by Nexiva Services

Kosh is a wallet for your ID cards and documents that keeps everything on your phone. It has no account, no cloud copy of your documents and no ads. This policy explains what stays on your phone, the few things that leave it, and why.

What Kosh keeps on your phone

The documents you add (photos, scans and PDFs), the details read from them or typed in (names, numbers, dates, addresses), family members, vehicles and service logs, emergency information and your settings.

All of this is stored in Kosh's private storage, encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The encryption key is itself locked by the Android Keystore and can only be used right after you unlock Kosh with your fingerprint or your phone's PIN, pattern or password. Android's cloud backup is turned off for Kosh, so none of this is copied to Google Drive.

If you turn on Emergency info on lock screen, that information (and only that: your name, blood group, allergies, conditions, medication and emergency contacts) is shown on your phone's lock screen as a notification, so whoever helps you can read it without unlocking. To show it again after the phone restarts, Kosh keeps that text outside the vault. Anyone holding your phone can see it, which is the point. Turning the setting off removes it.

The home-screen widget shows no documents or personal details, only shortcuts. Every one of them opens Kosh, which still asks you to unlock it.

Reading your documents (text recognition, barcode reading, and the optional voice and AI features) happens on your phone. Your documents, their text and your voice are never sent to us or to anyone else by Kosh.

What you choose to share

Kosh sends nothing on your behalf. When you tap Share, Share a stamped copy, Create backup or Add to calendar, Android hands what you picked to the app you choose (a messaging app, email, files, your calendar). From then on, that app's privacy policy applies.

  • Stamped copies carry a watermark with the purpose and the date.
  • Shared details are only the ones you tick, and the number can go out with only its last 4 characters.
  • Backups are encrypted with a passphrase you set. We can't open them.
  • Calendar events contain the card's name and its expiry date, never its number. Your calendar app may sync them to its own cloud.

Links you tap (for example DigiLocker, Parivahan or e-Challan) open in your browser. Kosh is not affiliated with those services or with any government.

Permissions

  • Location (optional): driving speed and the hospital check above.
  • Physical activity (optional): notices when you're in a vehicle, so your licence and vehicle papers come first.
  • Microphone (optional): voice search, transcribed on your phone.
  • Notifications: expiry and service reminders, scheduled on your phone.
  • Biometrics: unlocking Kosh.
  • Camera: Kosh doesn't ask for it. Scanning goes through Google's document scanner, which uses the camera for Kosh and returns only the pages you keep.

What we collect

We, the developer, don't run servers for Kosh and don't receive your documents, your details, your location or any analytics from the app. We have no copy of your data to hand over, sell or lose.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays on your phone until you delete it. You can delete single cards in Kosh, erase everything with Settings → Erase all data, or uninstall Kosh. There's no account to close and no copy held anywhere else, except backups you created yourself.

Children

Kosh is meant for adults managing their own and their family's documents. It isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect any information from children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date, and significant changes will be noted in the app's release notes.
